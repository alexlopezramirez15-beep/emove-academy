THINK!

Teach Students How to Think, Not What to Think

The Future Doesn’t Need Students Who Know What to Think. It Needs People Who Know How to Think.

Students have access to more information than any generation before them.

Answers are everywhere.

Search engines can find them.

Algorithms can recommend them.

Artificial Intelligence can generate them in seconds.

But having access to answers does not mean knowing how to think.

Students need to learn how to:

question information, examine evidence, consider alternatives, generate ideas, make decisions, solve problems and reflect on their own thinking.

THINK! is a practical course designed to help educators make thinking visible, intentional and teachable.

Because thinking should not simply happen somewhere in the background of a lesson.

It can become part of what we teach.

⸻

WHY THIS COURSE?

Teachers ask students to think every day.

Think about the answer.

Think carefully.

Think of another solution.

What do you think?

But there is a problem.

We often ask students to think without explicitly teaching them how.

What does it actually mean to analyse something?

How do you evaluate evidence?

How do you generate alternatives?

How do you recognise an assumption?

How do you make a good decision?

How do you know whether your first idea is actually your best one?

THINK! gives educators practical tools to transform thinking from an invisible expectation into an explicit part of learning.

⸻

THE THINK APPROACH

Throughout the course, participants work with the THINK framework.

T · TUNE IN

Stop. Notice. Question.

Thinking begins before answering.

Participants explore strategies that help students slow down, observe carefully and activate curiosity.

Students learn to ask:

* What do I notice?
* What do I already know?
* What am I assuming?
* What surprises me?
* What questions do I have?
* What might I be missing?

Before searching for the answer, we create a reason to think.

⸻

H · HUNT FOR EVIDENCE

Don’t just have an opinion. Build one.

Students encounter claims constantly.

From teachers.

From friends.

From social media.

From websites.

From influencers.

From Artificial Intelligence.

Critical thinking requires learning to ask:

How do I know?

Participants explore how to help students:

* Identify claims
* Look for evidence
* Distinguish fact from opinion
* Question assumptions
* Compare information
* Identify weak reasoning
* Justify conclusions
* Change their mind when evidence changes

⸻

I · IMAGINE ALTERNATIVES

The first idea is not always the best idea.

Thinking is not only critical.

It is also creative.

Participants explore strategies that help students move beyond the obvious and generate:

* Multiple ideas
* Different perspectives
* Alternative explanations
* New possibilities
* Unexpected connections
* Different solutions

Students learn that creativity is not simply “being artistic”.

It is the ability to think:

WHAT ELSE COULD BE POSSIBLE?

⸻

N · NAVIGATE DECISIONS

Good thinking helps us choose.

Real life rarely gives us multiple-choice answers.

Students need to evaluate possibilities, consider consequences and make decisions when there may not be one perfect solution.

Participants explore:

* Decision-making
* Problem-solving
* Criteria
* Priorities
* Consequences
* Trade-offs
* Different perspectives
* Ethical dilemmas
* Real-world problems

Students learn to move from:

“I choose this.”

to:

“I choose this because…”

⸻

K · KNOW YOUR THINKING

Think about how you think.

The final dimension is metacognition.

Students become stronger learners when they can recognise:

* What strategies they used
* What was difficult
* What changed their mind
* Where they made a mistake
* What helped them understand
* What they would do differently next time

The goal is not only to solve today’s problem.

It is to become better equipped for tomorrow’s problem.

⸻

FIVE DIMENSIONS OF POWERFUL THINKING

THINK! brings together five complementary areas:

VISIBLE THINKING
Making thought processes observable and discussable.

CRITICAL THINKING
Questioning, analysing, evaluating and using evidence.

CREATIVE THINKING
Generating alternatives, connections and possibilities.

DECISION-MAKING & PROBLEM-SOLVING
Evaluating options and responding to complex situations.

METACOGNITION
Understanding and improving our own thinking.

Together, they create learners who do more than remember information.

They learn how to use their minds deliberately.

Participants explore practical strategies for helping learners question claims and build conclusions based on evidence.

We explore:

* Claims
* Evidence
* Reasoning
* Fact vs. opinion
* Assumptions
* Perspective
* Reliability
* Bias
* Arguments
* Questioning information
* Changing conclusions

A central question guides the day:

HOW DO YOU KNOW?

Daily challenge:

Investigate a claim and build an evidence-based conclusion.

⸻

DAY 3 · WHAT ELSE?

Unlocking creative thinking

Today the objective is to escape the first obvious answer.

Participants experience creative-thinking techniques designed to generate possibilities and new connections.

We explore:

* Divergent thinking
* Convergent thinking
* Brainstorming
* Alternative uses
* Perspective shifts
* Constraints
* Connections
* Idea generation
* Creative problem-solving
* Moving beyond the first answer

Students should learn that when they have one idea, thinking may only have started.

DAY 4 · YOU DECIDE

Problems, choices and consequences

* Trade-offs
* Priorities
* Ethical dilemmas
* Perspective-taking
* Collaborative decision-making
* Justifying decisions

The objective is not simply to reach the “correct” answer.

We explore:

* Metacognition

BUILD YOUR THINKING CLASSROOM

DON’T GIVE THEM THE ANSWER TOO QUICKLY.

Silence can feel uncomfortable.

A student asks a question.

The teacher knows the answer.

It is tempting to provide it immediately.

But sometimes the most powerful response is another question.

What do you think?

What makes you say that?

What evidence do you have?

Could there be another explanation?

Does anyone see it differently?

What would change your mind?

How could we find out?

THINK! explores how teachers can move from being primarily:

ANSWER PROVIDERS

towards becoming:

THINKING DESIGNERS.

⸻

FROM ANSWER CULTURE TO THINKING CULTURE

A classroom focused mainly on answers asks:

“What’s the correct answer?”

A thinking classroom also asks:

“How did you get there?”

“What evidence supports it?”

“What else could be true?”

“Who sees it differently?”

“What strategy did you use?”

“What changed your mind?”

“What would you try next?”

Correct answers still matter.

But the path towards them matters too.

⸻

MISTAKES BECOME THINKING MATERIAL.

When students make a mistake, the most interesting question is not always:

“What is the correct answer?”

Sometimes it is:

“What happened in your thinking?”

Error analysis can help students identify:

* Assumptions
* Misunderstandings
* Missing information
* Ineffective strategies
* Alternative approaches

The mistake becomes evidence.

Not evidence that the student cannot think.

Evidence that helps them understand how they thought.

⸻

THINKING NEEDS TIME.

Fast answers can sometimes look impressive.

But speed is not the same as depth.

Participants explore classroom structures that create space for students to:

PAUSE → OBSERVE → QUESTION → DISCUSS → RECONSIDER → RESPOND

Students should know that changing their mind is not weakness.

When new evidence appears, changing your conclusion can be a sign of better thinking.
