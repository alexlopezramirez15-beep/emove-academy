EVERY STUDENT CAN SHINE

Turn Learning Differences into Opportunities for Success

Different Learners. Different Paths. Same Right to Shine.

There is no such thing as an average classroom.

Some students need more time.

Some need movement.

Some need visual support.

Some struggle to read, write, focus or organise themselves.

Some learn incredibly quickly.

Some communicate differently.

Some need help that is obvious.

Others need help that nobody sees.

Every Student Can Shine is a practical course designed to help educators create more inclusive learning environments where differences are not treated as problems to fix, but as realities to understand and respond to.

The goal is not to create 25 different lessons for 25 different students.

It is to design learning with enough flexibility, accessibility and support so that more students can participate, progress and experience success.

⸻

WHY THIS COURSE?

Inclusive education is easy to support in principle.

The challenge begins when there are 25 or 30 different learners sitting in front of you.

One student finishes everything immediately.

Another does not understand the instructions.

One cannot stay focused.

Another understands the content but struggles to express it in writing.

One needs constant reassurance.

Another becomes overwhelmed.

And the teacher still has one classroom, one timetable and limited time.

That is where inclusion needs to become practical.

Educators need realistic strategies to:

* Understand learning differences
* Identify barriers to participation
* Design more accessible learning experiences
* Differentiate without multiplying workload
* Support attention and executive functioning
* Make instructions clearer
* Provide appropriate scaffolding
* Offer different ways to participate
* Respond to different learning rhythms
* Create emotionally safe classrooms
* Maintain high expectations while providing support
* Help students recognise their own strengths

Inclusion is not about lowering expectations.

It is about creating different pathways towards meaningful learning.

THE SHINE APPROACH

Throughout the course, participants work with the SHINE framework, a practical model for inclusive classroom design.

S · SEE THE LEARNER

Look beyond the behaviour.

Before deciding what a student needs, we need to understand what might be happening.

Participants learn to observe students through a different lens.

Instead of asking:

“What’s wrong with this student?”

we begin asking:

“What might be making this difficult?”

We explore strengths, needs, learning preferences, barriers, context and student voice.

⸻

H · HUNT FOR BARRIERS

Sometimes the problem is not the learner.

A long instruction.

A crowded worksheet.

An inflexible task.

Too much information at once.

Only one way to demonstrate learning.

An environment full of distractions.

Small elements of learning design can create significant barriers.

Participants learn to identify them before immediately assuming that the difficulty belongs to the student.

⸻

I · INCLUDE FROM THE START

Design for diversity, not for the imaginary average student.

Instead of designing one lesson and adapting it afterwards for students who struggle, participants explore how flexibility can be incorporated from the beginning.

We work with:

* Multiple ways of presenting information
* Different ways of participating
* Flexible resources
* Visual support
* Choice
* Scaffolding
* Accessible instructions
* Different ways of demonstrating learning

⸻

N · NURTURE POTENTIAL

Support should help students move forward.

Inclusion is not about making everything easier.

Students need appropriate challenge.

Participants explore how to provide the right support at the right moment while gradually developing independence.

The objective is:

SUPPORT → PROGRESS → AUTONOMY

⸻

E · EMPOWER EVERY LEARNER

Students need to understand how they learn.

True inclusion does not end with teacher adaptation.

Students can learn to:

* Recognise their strengths
* Understand what helps them
* Ask for support
* Make choices
* Use strategies
* Reflect on their learning
* Advocate for their needs
* Celebrate progress

Because every student deserves to feel:

“There is a place for me here.”

5 DAYS TO BUILD A CLASSROOM WHERE EVERYONE CAN PARTICIPATE

DAY 1 · SEE ME

Understanding the learner behind the label

The course begins by challenging the idea of the “average learner”.

Participants explore the diversity that already exists in every classroom.

We explore:

* Learner variability
* Strengths and needs
* Neurodiversity
* Learning differences
* Labels and assumptions
* Behaviour as communication
* Student voice
* Hidden barriers
* Strength-based approaches

We explore common profiles and needs without reducing students to diagnoses.

Daily challenge:

Create a Learner Portrait that begins with strengths rather than difficulties.

⸻

DAY 2 · REMOVE THE BARRIERS

What is making learning difficult?

Participants analyse everyday classroom situations to identify barriers that can unintentionally exclude learners.

We explore:

* Cognitive load
* Complex instructions
* Classroom environment
* Visual overload
* Attention demands
* Reading and writing demands
* Time pressure
* Participation barriers
* Sensory considerations
* Rigid task structures

A central question guides the day:

WHAT CAN WE CHANGE AROUND THE LEARNER?

Daily challenge:

Take an ordinary classroom activity and identify the invisible barriers inside it.

⸻

DAY 3 · DESIGN FOR EVERYONE

Inclusion begins before the lesson starts

Today participants redesign learning experiences to make them accessible to a wider range of students from the beginning.

We explore:

* Inclusive lesson design
* Universal Design for Learning principles
* Multiple means of engagement
* Multiple ways of accessing information
* Multiple ways of demonstrating learning
* Visual supports
* Choice
* Flexible grouping
* Accessible materials
* Clear instructions

Daily challenge:

Transform a traditional activity into an Inclusive Learning Experience.

⸻

DAY 4 · SUPPORT WITHOUT LIMITING

Different support. High expectations.

Students do not all need the same support.

And support should not become permanent dependence.

Participants explore practical differentiation and scaffolding strategies that can work in real classrooms.

We explore:

* Scaffolding
* Differentiation
* Task complexity
* Chunking
* Modelling
* Worked examples
* Checklists
* Visual organisers
* Flexible timing
* Support for attention
* Executive functioning
* Student autonomy
* Gradual removal of support

The key question becomes:

“What is the smallest support this learner needs to move forward?”

Daily challenge:

Build a Scaffolding Ladder for a real learning task.

⸻

DAY 5 · LET THEM SHINE

From participation to belonging

Inclusion is not complete simply because students can access an activity.

They also need to feel valued.

The final day focuses on belonging, strengths, autonomy and classroom culture.

We explore:

* Sense of belonging
* Student confidence
* Strength-based feedback
* Classroom relationships
* Peer support
* Student voice and choice
* Self-advocacy
* Learning strategies
* Celebrating progress
* Building an inclusive classroom culture

Final challenge:

MY SHINE CLASSROOM PLAN

Each participant designs a realistic action plan for making their own classroom or educational context more inclusive.

DIFFERENTIATION WITHOUT 25 LESSON PLANS

One of the biggest concerns teachers have about inclusion is workload.

If every learner is different, does the teacher need to prepare a different activity for everyone?

No.

The course focuses on sustainable differentiation.

Participants learn to modify key elements such as:

ACCESS · SUPPORT · PROCESS · CHALLENGE · PRODUCT · TIME · CHOICE

rather than creating completely separate lessons.

One learning objective.

Multiple ways to reach it.

⸻

FROM “DIFFICULT STUDENT” TO “DIFFICULT SITUATION”

Language influences how we respond.

Throughout the course, participants practise changing the questions they ask.

Instead of:

“Why won’t they concentrate?”

Ask:

“What is competing for their attention?”

Instead of:

“Why won’t they start?”

Ask:

“What is making the first step difficult?”

Instead of:

“Why are they behaving like this?”

Ask:

“What might this behaviour be communicating?”

Instead of:

“Why can’t they do what everyone else does?”

Ask:

“What barrier might be preventing them from participating?”

Changing the question can change the response.
